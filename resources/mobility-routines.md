# Mobility & Recovery Routines

**Purpose**: Injury prevention, recovery enhancement, movement quality improvement

**Target Frequency**:
- Pre-run dynamic warm-up: Every run (5-10 min)
- Post-run stretching: Minimum 3x/week, ideally after every run (5-10 min)
- Standalone deep mobility: 1-2x/week (20-30 min)

---

## Pre-Run Dynamic Warm-Up (5-10 minutes)

**When**: Before every run, especially before workouts
**Goal**: Increase blood flow, activate muscles, prepare joints for running

### Routine (complete in order)

**1. Walking Leg Swings** (30 seconds each direction)
- Hold onto wall or stable surface for balance
- Forward/backward swings: Swing leg forward and back, relaxed and controlled
- Side-to-side swings: Swing leg across body and out to side
- Keep torso stable, let leg swing freely
- Purpose: Hip mobility, dynamic range of motion

**2. Walking Lunges** (10-12 reps per leg)
- Step forward into lunge (both knees at 90°)
- Back knee hovers just above ground
- Push through front heel to stand and step into next lunge
- Keep torso upright, core engaged
- Purpose: Hip flexor mobility, glute activation, balance

**3. High Knees March** (20-30 seconds)
- March in place, bringing knees up to hip height
- Controlled tempo (not fast)
- Engage core, maintain tall posture
- Drive arms in running motion
- Purpose: Hip flexor activation, running-specific warm-up

**4. Butt Kicks** (20-30 seconds)
- Jog in place, bringing heels up toward glutes
- Controlled tempo
- Light on feet, quick turnover
- Purpose: Hamstring activation, running movement prep

**5. Side Shuffles** (20 seconds each direction)
- Shuffle laterally, staying low in athletic stance
- Don't cross feet, maintain wide base
- Purpose: Hip abductor activation, lateral stability

**6. Calf Raises** (15-20 reps)
- Rise up on toes, hold briefly at top
- Lower slowly with control
- Can hold wall for balance
- Purpose: Ankle/calf activation, lower leg prep

**7. A-Skips** (20-30 seconds) *[Optional - add for hard workouts]*
- Skip forward with exaggerated knee drive
- Focus on vertical lift, powerful toe-off
- Arms drive opposite to legs
- Purpose: Running power, coordination, warm-up intensity

**Total Time**: 5-7 minutes (add A-skips for 8-10 min before hard workouts)

## Post-Run Static Stretching & Foam Rolling (5-10 minutes)

**When**: After every run (minimum 3x/week), when muscles are warm
**Goal**: Reduce muscle tightness, improve flexibility, aid recovery

### Stretching Routine (hold each 30-45 seconds per side)

**1. Standing Quad Stretch**
- Stand on one leg, pull opposite foot toward glutes
- Keep knees together, hips neutral (don't arch back)
- Hold wall for balance if needed
- Feel stretch in front of thigh
- **30-45 seconds per leg**

**2. Standing Hamstring Stretch**
- Place one heel on low step or curb
- Keep leg straight, flex foot toward shin
- Hinge forward from hips (keep back straight)
- Feel stretch in back of thigh
- **30-45 seconds per leg**

**3. Standing Calf Stretch**
- Step one foot back, keep heel down
- Front knee bent, back leg straight
- Lean forward into wall or tree
- Feel stretch in calf of back leg
- **30-45 seconds per leg**

**4. Hip Flexor Stretch** (low lunge position)
- Kneel on one knee, other foot forward (90° angle)
- Push hips forward gently
- Keep torso upright, slight posterior pelvic tilt
- Feel stretch in front of hip of kneeling leg
- **30-45 seconds per leg**

**5. Pigeon Pose** (or Figure-4 Stretch)
- Sit on ground, one leg bent in front, other extended behind
- OR lying on back, ankle of one leg crossed over opposite knee
- Feel stretch in glute/hip of bent leg
- **30-45 seconds per leg**

**6. Seated Spinal Twist**
- Sit with one leg straight, cross other leg over
- Rotate torso toward bent knee
- Use opposite arm on outside of bent knee for leverage
- Feel stretch in lower back and glute
- **30-45 seconds per side**

### Foam Rolling Routine (1-2 minutes per area)

**Focus Areas**:
1. **IT Band**: Lie on side, roll from hip to just above knee (avoid rolling directly on knee joint)
2. **Quads**: Lie face down, roll from hip to just above knee
3. **Calves**: Sit with legs extended, roll from ankle to just below knee
4. **Glutes**: Sit on roller, cross one ankle over opposite knee, roll glute area

**Foam Rolling Tips**:
- Roll slowly (1 inch per second)
- Pause on tender spots for 20-30 seconds
- Breathe deeply, don't hold breath
- Avoid rolling directly over joints or bones
- Pain should be 4-6/10 (uncomfortable but tolerable)

**Total Time**: 5-10 minutes (3-5 min stretching + 2-5 min foam rolling)

## Standalone Deep Mobility Session (20-30 minutes)

**When**: 1-2x per week (Sunday recommended), separate from running
**Goal**: Deep tissue work, address chronic tightness, maintain joint health

### Session Structure

**Part 1: Foam Rolling & Myofascial Release** (10-12 minutes)

**1. Calves** (2 min each leg)
- Sit with leg extended on roller
- Roll from Achilles to just below knee
- Rotate leg inward and outward to hit different angles
- Pause on tender spots

**2. IT Band** (2 min each leg)
- Lie on side, roller between hip and knee
- Roll slowly, pause on tender areas
- Can cross top leg over for more pressure

**3. Quads** (2 min each leg)
- Lie face down, roller under thighs
- Roll from hip to just above knee
- Rotate leg to hit all quad muscles

**4. Glutes/Piriformis** (2 min each side)
- Sit on roller, shift weight to one side
- Can cross ankle over opposite knee for deeper pressure
- Roll slowly, breathe through tender areas

**5. Upper Back/Thoracic Spine** (2 min)
- Lie on back, roller under upper back
- Support head with hands
- Roll from mid-back to shoulders (avoid lower back)
- Can extend arms overhead for deeper stretch

**Part 2: Deep Stretching** (10-15 minutes)

**1. Low Lunge with Quad Stretch** (90 seconds per side)
- Start in low lunge (back knee on ground)
- Reach back and grab back foot, pull toward glute
- Feel stretch in hip flexor AND quad of back leg
- Keep torso upright, don't lean forward

**2. Pigeon Pose** (90 seconds per side)
- From hands and knees, bring one shin forward (parallel to front of mat)
- Extend other leg straight behind
- Lower down onto forearms or forehead to mat
- Deep glute/hip stretch
- Breathe into the stretch, relax

**3. Seated Forward Fold** (90 seconds)
- Sit with both legs extended straight
- Hinge forward from hips (not rounding back)
- Reach toward toes (or shins if tight)
- Feel stretch in hamstrings and lower back

**4. Supine Figure-4 Stretch** (90 seconds per side)
- Lie on back, both knees bent, feet on ground
- Cross ankle of one leg over opposite knee
- Pull thigh of bottom leg toward chest
- Feel stretch in glute/hip of crossed leg

**5. Supine Spinal Twist** (90 seconds per side)
- Lie on back, extend arms out to sides
- Drop both knees to one side
- Keep shoulders on ground
- Feel stretch in lower back and obliques

**6. Hip Flexor Couch Stretch** (90 seconds per side)
- Face away from couch/wall, place one shin on couch behind you
- Other foot forward in lunge position
- Upright torso, push hips forward
- Very deep hip flexor stretch (crucial for runners)

**7. Child's Pose** (90 seconds)
- Kneel, sit back on heels, extend arms forward
- Forehead to ground
- Full body relaxation, deep breathing
- Gentle stretch in hips, back, shoulders

**Part 3: Activation & Strengthening** (5 minutes)

**1. Clamshells** (15 reps per side)
- Lie on side, knees bent, feet together
- Open top knee like clamshell (keep feet together)
- Purpose: Glute medius activation (knee stability)

**2. Glute Bridges** (15 reps)
- Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat
- Lift hips to create straight line from knees to shoulders
- Squeeze glutes at top, hold 2 seconds
- Purpose: Glute activation, posterior chain

**3. Bird Dogs** (10 reps per side)
- Hands and knees position
- Extend opposite arm and leg
- Hold 3 seconds, maintain stable core
- Purpose: Core stability, balance

**Total Time**: 25-30 minutes

## Targeted Mobility for Specific Issues

### For Tight Hip Flexors (common in runners)

**Daily Routine** (5 minutes):
1. Kneeling hip flexor stretch: 60 seconds per side
2. Leg swings (forward/back): 20 per side
3. Walking lunges: 10 per side

**Why It Matters**: Tight hip flexors alter running mechanics, increase knee stress

### For IT Band Tightness

**Daily Routine** (5 minutes):
1. IT band foam rolling: 2 min per side
2. Standing IT band stretch: 45 seconds per side
3. Clamshells: 15 per side

**Why It Matters**: IT band tightness can contribute to knee pain

### For Calf Tightness

**Daily Routine** (5 minutes):
1. Calf foam rolling: 2 min per side
2. Standing calf stretch (straight leg): 45 seconds per side
3. Calf stretch (bent knee, soleus focus): 45 seconds per side

**Why It Matters**: Tight calves limit ankle mobility, increase Achilles stress

### For Lower Back Tightness

**Daily Routine** (5 minutes):
1. Cat-cow stretches: 10 reps
2. Child's pose: 90 seconds
3. Supine spinal twist: 60 seconds per side

**Why It Matters**: Running can cause lower back stiffness from repetitive impact

## Mobility Integration with Training Schedule

### Standard Week Template

**Monday** (Easy Run + HSR + Sauna):
- Pre-run: 5 min dynamic warm-up
- Post-run: 5 min stretching
- Post-HSR: 5 min targeted stretching (hips/quads)

**Tuesday** (Easy Run + Mobility + Sauna):
- Pre-run: 5 min dynamic warm-up
- Post-run: 10 min stretching + foam rolling

**Wednesday** (Workout Day + Sauna):
- Pre-run: 8-10 min dynamic warm-up (include A-skips)
- Post-run: 10 min stretching + foam rolling (extra attention)

**Thursday** (Easy Run + HSR + Sauna):
- Pre-run: 5 min dynamic warm-up
- Post-run: 5 min stretching

**Friday** (Rest or Easy Run + Sauna):
- If running: 5 min pre-run warm-up, 5 min post-run stretch
- If rest: Optional 10 min stretching or foam rolling

**Saturday** (Long Run + Sauna):
- Pre-run: 8-10 min dynamic warm-up (especially important)
- Post-run: 10 min stretching + foam rolling (crucial for recovery)

**Sunday** (Active Recovery + Mobility):
- **20-30 min standalone deep mobility session** (see above)
- Focus on full body recovery

## Tips for Consistency

### Making It Stick
1. **Pre-run warm-up**: Do it BEFORE you start your watch. Make it non-negotiable.
2. **Post-run stretch**: Immediately after runs, while still in running clothes. Don't sit down first.
3. **Sunday mobility**: Schedule it like a workout. Calendar block it.
4. **Foam rolling**: Keep foam roller visible (near where you take shoes off).

### Minimum Effective Dose
If time is limited, prioritize:
1. Pre-run dynamic warm-up (ALWAYS)
2. Post-run hip flexor + calf stretch (3 minutes)
3. Weekly deep mobility session (20 min minimum)

### Signs You Need More Mobility Work
- Knee pain increasing
- Feeling "tight" during runs
- Difficulty maintaining form in later miles
- Chronic low back discomfort
- Shortened stride length

## Mobility & Knee Health

**Specific to Patellar Tendinopathy Management**:

**Critical Areas to Address**:
1. **Hip Flexors**: Tight hip flexors increase quad demand
2. **Quads**: Direct impact on patellar tendon load
3. **IT Band**: Can alter knee tracking
4. **Calves**: Affect overall lower limb biomechanics
5. **Glutes**: Weakness leads to compensation patterns

**Daily Knee-Focused Routine** (5 minutes):
- Hip flexor stretch: 60s per side
- Quad stretch: 45s per side
- IT band foam rolling: 90s per side
- Clamshells: 15 per side (activation)

**Monitor**: If knee pain is >3/10, add extra mobility session that day

## Questions & Adjustments

**"I don't have a foam roller"**:
- Use tennis ball or lacrosse ball for targeted areas
- Perform extra static stretching instead
- Foam roller recommended purchase (~$20-30)

**"I'm too tight to do pigeon pose"**:
- Start with figure-4 stretch (lying down version)
- Use yoga block or pillow under hip in pigeon
- Flexibility improves over weeks - be patient

**"When should I do mobility if I run twice a day?"**:
- Dynamic warm-up before each run
- Stretching after second run only
- Deep mobility on rest days

**"Can I do too much mobility work?"**:
- Difficult to overdo, but prioritize running recovery
- If mobility work causes fatigue that impacts runs, scale back
- Listen to your body

---

**Remember**: Mobility work is training, not extra. It's injury prevention, recovery enhancement, and performance optimization. Consistency beats intensity - 5 minutes daily is better than 60 minutes once.
