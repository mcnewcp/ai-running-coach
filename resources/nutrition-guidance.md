# Endurance Nutrition Guidance

A generic fueling framework: during-run fueling, hydration, electrolytes, pre- and post-run nutrition, and tendon-supportive eating.

**The Runner Profile comes first.** Read it for the runner's food preferences, supplements, body weight and any medical condition that affects diet. A condition managed through diet (sodium, sugar, caffeine or anything else) overrides the defaults here: flag any conflict and have the runner clear a meaningful change with their clinician before adopting it. A race-specific fueling plan belongs in that Program's folder in the vault, not here.

---

## Guiding principles

1. **Whole foods first.** Gels, drinks and powders are tools for specific sessions, not the foundation. The everyday diet is real food.
2. **Fuel the work required.** Easy short runs need little; long or hard sessions need more. Don't over-fuel easy days or under-fuel hard ones.
3. **Add sodium deliberately.** Replace electrolytes when sweat losses are real, sized to the runner's needs and health.
4. **Protein supports recovery and tendons,** especially during strength or rehab Phases.
5. **Train the gut.** Practise race fueling in training so the stomach tolerates carbohydrate on the move before it's needed.
6. **Don't under-fuel overall.** A chronic energy deficit raises the risk of injury, illness and tendon flare-ups.

## During-run fueling (carbohydrate)

Decide by **duration**, not distance:

| Run duration | Carbohydrate during the run | Practical guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **< 75 min** | None needed | Muscle glycogen and a normal pre-run meal cover it. Most easy runs land here. |
| **75–90 min** | Optional (0–30 g) | One gel mid-run if wanted, or to practise fueling. |
| **90 min – 2.5 hr** | **30–60 g/hr** | About 1.5–2.5 gels an hour, one every 25–40 min. A single carbohydrate source (glucose or maltodextrin) is fine. |
| **2.5 hr+ or racing** | **60–90 g/hr** | Needs a trained gut and **multiple carbohydrate types** (glucose:fructose about 2:1) to absorb that much. |

**Gel mechanics:**

- A typical gel is about 22–25 g of carbohydrate.
- **Take gels with a few sips of water.** Taken dry, or chased with a sports drink, they concentrate sugar in the gut and cause GI distress.
- **Skip one at the start.** Glycogen covers the early miles: take the first gel 30–45 min in, before a dip, then re-dose on the cadence above.

**The fat-oxidation case for skipping fuel:** easy Zone 2 long runs partly train fat oxidation and aerobic efficiency. Running some of them with low carbohydrate availability (no mid-run fuel, or before breakfast) nudges that adaptation. Use it **selectively, on easy runs only**: always fuel quality workouts, long hard efforts and races.

## Pre-run fueling

| Before the run | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **2–4 hr** | A meal with carbohydrate, moderate protein, low fat and fibre | The fueling that matters for runs up to about 90 min. About 1–2 g carbohydrate per kg. |
| **30–60 min** | Optional light carbohydrate snack (banana, toast, half a gel) | Useful for early-morning runs, or if the last meal was a while ago. |

- **Run timing matters.** A runner who trains later in the day is usually fueled from earlier meals; an early-morning runner may need the snack. Check when the runner usually trains in the Runner Profile.
- **Fasted easy runs** (short, Zone 2) are fine and can support fat adaptation. Never run hard or long fasted.

## Hydration

- **Daily baseline:** aim for pale-yellow urine. Starting a run hydrated beats catching up mid-run.
- **Before:** about 16–20 oz of fluid in the 2–3 hours before, and a few sips about 15 min before.
- **During:** drink to thirst. For runs over 60 min or in heat, roughly **16–28 oz an hour**, adjusted to sweat and conditions.
- **Heat:** heat raises heart rate and perceived effort at any pace; that is expected, not lost fitness. On Zone 2 runs, **let heart rate govern and slow down**. A treadmill indoors is a legitimate way to hold easy effort on brutal days.
- **Optional sweat-rate check:** weigh before and after a longer run. Each lb lost is about 16 oz of fluid. Keep losses under about 2% of body weight, and use the number to set future intake.

---

## Electrolytes and sodium

- **Runs under 60–75 min:** no added electrolytes needed; plain water.
- **Long, hot or heavy-sweat sessions:** sweat sodium losses become real, and some replacement helps performance and cramping. Sports drinks, salt tabs and gels vary widely in sodium, so read labels and size intake to the runner's sweat losses.
- Sodium is one of several electrolytes; potassium and magnesium (from whole foods) matter too.
- **If the Runner Profile records a condition affected by sodium,** keep added sodium low-to-moderate and have the runner agree regular use of sports drinks or salt tabs with their clinician.

---

## Post-run and recovery nutrition

- **After hard or long sessions:** carbohydrate and protein within about 1–2 hours: roughly **20–40 g protein** plus carbohydrate to restock glycogen. A protein shake with fruit works well.
- **After easy short runs:** no special protocol; eat the next normal meal.
- **Daily protein:** about **1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight per day**, spread across 3–4 feedings of 30–40 g for the best muscle and tendon support, especially during strength or rehab Phases. Work out the grams from the runner's current body weight.

---

## Tendon-supportive nutrition (during rehab)

- **Enough total protein, evenly spread** (above), supports collagen turnover.
- **Emerging evidence:** about **15 g collagen or gelatin with about 50 mg vitamin C**, taken **30–60 min before** a tendon-loading (HSR) session, may boost collagen synthesis. Low risk and possibly helpful: a reasonable experiment during a rehab Phase, not essential.
- **Vitamin C and overall micronutrient adequacy** (a whole-foods diet covers most) support connective tissue.

---

## Daily and general

- **Whole foods first:** quality carbohydrate, lean protein, fruit and vegetables, healthy fats.
- **Carbohydrate to match training load:** don't chronically under-fuel higher-volume Phases.
- **Micronutrients runners should watch:** iron (low ferritin is common in endurance athletes; check bloodwork if energy lags), calcium and vitamin D (bone health), omega-3s.
- **Caffeine (optional):** about 3–6 mg per kg, 45–60 min before a hard session or race, is a proven ergogenic aid. It isn't needed for easy runs. Large doses bring jitters and poor sleep, so start modest, and less if the Runner Profile calls for it.

---

## Race-day fueling

- **Rehearse in training.** Never try a new fueling plan on race day.
- **Longer races:** a modest carbohydrate emphasis in the 1–2 days before (topping off glycogen, not stuffing), plus a practised during-race plan from the duration table above.
- **Each race gets its own fueling plan** in that Program's folder in the vault, built and rehearsed in the final weeks of the build.

## Common questions

**"Do I need a gel for my long run?"** Only if it's longer than about 75–90 min, or you're practising fueling for longer runs and races. Under about 75 min, glycogen and the pre-run meal have it covered.

**"Should I use a sports drink for electrolytes?"** Not for short runs. For long or hot sessions, a sports drink sized to your sweat losses is reasonable, unless a health condition says otherwise. Water handles most training.

**"Can I run fasted?"** Short easy runs, yes, and it can help fat adaptation. Long or hard sessions need fuel in the tank.

**"How much protein?"** About 1.6–2.2 g per kg a day, spread across the day. A protein shake is a convenient way to get there, especially after a session.
