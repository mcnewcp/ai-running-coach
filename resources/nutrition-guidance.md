# Endurance Nutrition Guidance

Durable, program-agnostic fueling framework for Coy McNew. Covers during-run fueling, hydration, electrolytes, pre/post nutrition, and tendon-supportive eating. Tailored to his profile: **whole-foods preference, hereditary high blood pressure managed with a low-sodium diet, protein-shake supplementation, and training goals built on aerobic base + fat oxidation.**

Time-varying state (current weight, active block) lives in `CLAUDE.md` / `data/`. Race-specific fueling plans live in the relevant program folder, not here.

> **Medical note:** Coy manages hereditary high blood pressure with a low-sodium diet. The endurance world's default "salt everything" advice does **not** apply by default here. Run any meaningful increase in sodium intake (sports drinks, salt tabs, high-sodium gels) past his physician before adopting it.

---

## Guiding Principles

1. **Whole foods first.** Gels, drinks, and powders are tools for specific sessions — not the foundation. The everyday diet is real food.
2. **Fuel the work required.** Match intake to the session: easy short runs need little; long/hard sessions need more. Don't over-fuel easy days, don't under-fuel hard ones.
3. **Low-sodium aware.** BP management takes priority over generic electrolyte advice. Add sodium deliberately, not reflexively.
4. **Protein supports recovery *and* tendon.** Especially relevant during strength/rehab blocks.
5. **Train the gut.** Practice race fueling in training so the stomach tolerates carbs on the move before you actually need it.
6. **Don't under-fuel overall.** Chronic energy deficit raises injury, illness, and tendon-flare risk — the opposite of the goal.

## During-Run Fueling (carbohydrate)

The single most-asked question: *do I need a gel?* Decided by **duration**, not distance:

| Run duration | Carb during run | Practical guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **< 75 min** | None needed | Muscle glycogen + a normal pre-run meal cover it. Most easy runs land here. |
| **75–90 min** | Optional (0–30 g) | One gel mid-run if desired or if practicing fueling. Not required. |
| **90 min – 2.5 hr** | **30–60 g/hr** | ~1.5–2.5 gels/hr, or one every ~25–40 min. Single carb source (glucose/maltodextrin) is fine. |
| **2.5 hr+ / racing** | **60–90 g/hr** | Requires a trained gut and **multiple carb types** (glucose:fructose ~2:1) to absorb that much. |

**Gel mechanics:**
- A typical gel = ~22–25 g carbs.
- **Always take gels with a few sips of water.** Gels need water to digest; taken dry — or chased with a sports drink — they concentrate sugar in the gut and cause GI distress.
- **Don't take one at the start.** You have glycogen early; first gel ~30–45 min in, before you'd feel a dip.
- Then re-dose on the cadence above for longer efforts.

**Fat-oxidation angle (the "why skip it" case):** Easy Z2 long runs are partly *for* training fat oxidation and aerobic efficiency. Running some of them with low carb availability (no mid-run fuel, or run before breakfast) nudges that adaptation. Use this **selectively on easy runs only** — always fully fuel quality workouts, long hard efforts, and races.

## Pre-Run Fueling

| Window before run | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **2–4 hr** | Meal with carbs, moderate protein, low fat/fiber | The fueling that actually matters for runs up to ~90 min. ~1–2 g carb/kg. |
| **30–60 min** | Optional light carb snack (banana, toast, ½ gel) | Useful for early-morning runs or if the last meal was a while ago. |

- **Midday runner advantage:** Coy runs 10am–2pm, so he's usually well-fueled from breakfast/lunch — pre-run fueling is rarely a problem. Just don't run on fumes from a skipped breakfast for anything long or hard.
- **Fasted easy runs** (short, Z2) are fine and can support fat adaptation. Don't run hard or long fasted.

## Hydration

- **Daily baseline:** aim for pale-yellow urine. Going into runs already hydrated beats trying to catch up mid-run.
- **Pre-run:** ~16–20 oz fluid in the 2–3 hr before; a few more sips ~15 min before.
- **During:** drink to thirst. For runs >60 min or in heat, ballpark **16–28 oz/hr**, adjusted to sweat and conditions.
- **Heat reality (TN summer):** heat raises HR and perceived effort at any pace — expected, not a fitness loss. On Z2 runs, **let HR govern and slow down**; the **treadmill/AC is a legitimate tool** to hold easy effort on brutal days.
- **Optional sweat-rate check:** weigh yourself before and after a longer run. Each lb lost ≈ 16 oz of fluid. Aim to keep losses under ~2% of body weight; use the number to dial in future intake.

## Electrolytes & Sodium (BP-specific)

This is where Coy's plan **deviates from generic endurance advice**:

- **Runs < 60–75 min:** no added electrolytes needed — plain water.
- **Long / hot / heavy-sweat sessions:** sweat sodium losses become real, so *some* electrolyte replacement can help performance and cramping. But:
  - **Choose low-to-moderate sodium options** and don't load up. Many endurance products are very high sodium — read labels.
  - Sodium is one of several electrolytes; potassium, magnesium (from whole foods) matter too.
- **The balance is individual and doctor-informed:** real sweat losses on long hot runs vs. hereditary HTN management. Discuss any regular use of sports drinks/salt tabs with his physician.

## Post-Run & Recovery Nutrition

- **After hard or long sessions:** carbs + protein within ~1–2 hr. Roughly **20–40 g protein** plus carbs to restock glycogen. A **protein shake + fruit/carb** fits perfectly (and is already his go-to supplement).
- **After easy short runs:** no special protocol — just eat your normal next meal.
- **Daily protein target:** ~**1.6–2.2 g/kg/day** ≈ **125–170 g/day** at ~171 lb. Spread across 3–4 feedings of ~30–40 g for best muscle/tendon support — especially important during strength/rehab blocks.

## Tendon-Supportive Nutrition (rehab-relevant)

Coy is managing patellar tendinopathy with HSR loading — nutrition can support the connective-tissue side:

- **Adequate total protein + even distribution** (above) supports collagen turnover.
- **Emerging evidence:** ~**15 g collagen or gelatin + ~50 mg vitamin C** taken **30–60 min before** a tendon-loading session (HSR) may boost collagen synthesis. Low risk, possibly helpful — a reasonable experiment during a rehab block. Optional, not essential.
- **Vitamin C and overall micronutrient adequacy** (whole-foods diet covers most) support connective tissue.

## Daily / General

- **Whole foods first** (aligns with preference): quality carbs, lean proteins, fruits/vegetables, healthy fats.
- **Carbohydrate to match training load** — don't chronically under-fuel during higher-volume blocks.
- **Micronutrients to watch for runners:** iron (fatigue/low ferritin is common in endurance athletes — check via bloodwork if energy lags), calcium + vitamin D (bone health), omega-3s.
- **Caffeine (optional):** ~3–6 mg/kg ~45–60 min pre hard session/race is a proven ergogenic. Not needed for easy runs. **Use modest amounts and stay BP-aware** — large doses can transiently raise blood pressure.

## Race-Day Fueling

- **Rehearse in training** — never try a new fueling plan on race day.
- **Longer races:** modest carbohydrate emphasis in the 1–2 days prior (top off glycogen — "fueling up," not stuffing), plus a practiced during-race plan per the duration table above.
- **Each race gets its own specific fueling plan** in that race's program folder, built and rehearsed in the final weeks of the build.

## Common Questions

**"Do I need a gel for my long run?"**
Only if it's longer than ~75–90 min, or you're practicing fueling for future longer runs/races. Under ~75 min, glycogen + your pre-run meal have it covered.

**"Should I use a sports drink for electrolytes?"**
Not for short runs. For long/hot sessions, a modest low-sodium option is reasonable — but given your BP, clear regular use with your doctor first. Water handles most of your training.

**"Can I run fasted?"**
Short easy runs, yes — and it can help fat adaptation. Don't run long or hard fasted; quality and long sessions need fuel in the tank.

**"How much protein?"**
~125–170 g/day, spread across the day. Your protein shake is a convenient way to hit it, especially post-session.
