# Heavy Slow Resistance (HSR) Training Protocols

**Purpose**: Tendon strengthening, injury prevention (patellar tendinopathy management), running-specific strength

**Frequency**: 2x per week (non-consecutive days)
**Duration**: 35-45 minutes per session
**Timing**: After easy runs or on rest days from running

---

## Why HSR Training for Runners?

### Benefits for Patellar Tendinopathy
- **Tendon Strengthening**: HSR loading stimulates collagen production and tendon remodeling
- **Load Tolerance**: Increases tendon capacity to handle running impact
- **Pain Reduction**: Evidence-based approach for managing tendon pain
- **Injury Prevention**: Strengthens kinetic chain to reduce knee stress

### Benefits for Running Performance
- **Power Development**: Improves force production for stronger push-off
- **Economy**: Better running economy from improved strength
- **Injury Resistance**: Addresses muscle imbalances and weaknesses
- **Joint Stability**: Strengthens stabilizer muscles around knee, hip, ankle

---

## HSR Training Principles

### Key Characteristics
1. **Heavy Load**: 70-85% of 1-rep max (or bodyweight equivalent)
2. **Slow Tempo**: 3-4 second eccentric (lowering), 1-2 second concentric (lifting)
3. **Low Reps**: 6-8 reps per set (quality over quantity)
4. **Moderate Volume**: 2-3 sets per exercise
5. **Progressive Overload**: Gradually increase load over weeks

### Tempo Notation
**Example: 3-1-3**
- 3 seconds: Eccentric (lowering phase)
- 1 second: Pause at bottom
- 3 seconds: Concentric (lifting phase)

## Exercise Descriptions & Cues

### 1. Warm-up Wall Sits

**Purpose**: Isometric quad activation, tendon prep, blood flow

**Setup**:
- Back against wall, feet shoulder-width apart
- Walk feet out so knees are at 90° when in squat position
- Thighs parallel to ground

**Execution**:
- Lower into squat position (90° knee angle)
- Hold for 45 seconds
- Focus on pressing back into wall, engaging quads
- Rest 90-120 seconds between sets

**Cues**:
- "Sit back like sitting in a chair"
- "Knees stay over ankles, don't let them drift forward"
- "Engage quads, feel the burn"

**Modifications**:
- If too difficult: Higher position (knees at 100-110°)
- If too easy: Single leg wall sit (advanced)

---

### 2. Eccentric Decline Squats (PRIMARY EXERCISE FOR TENDON LOADING)

**Purpose**: Specific patellar tendon loading, eccentric strengthening

**Setup**:
- Stand on decline board (~15-20° angle) or wedge
- Barbell across upper back (back squat position)
- Feet shoulder-width apart, toes forward

**Execution**:
- **3 seconds down**: Lower slowly into squat (90° knee angle)
- **1 second pause**: Hold at bottom position
- **3 seconds up**: Drive through heels to return to start
- Complete 8 reps x 2 sets

**Current Load**: 95 lbs

**Cues**:
- "Control the descent - count 1-2-3"
- "Chest up, core tight throughout"
- "Drive through heels on the way up"
- "Knees track over toes, don't cave inward"

**Modifications**:
- If knee pain >4/10: Reduce weight by 10-15 lbs
- If too easy: Add 5-10 lbs next session
- Alternative: Goblet squat on decline (hold DB at chest)

**Progression Plan** (weeks 1-16):
- Weeks 1-4: 95 lbs
- Weeks 5-8: 105 lbs
- Weeks 9-12: 115 lbs
- Weeks 13-16: Maintain 115 lbs (taper phase)

---

### 3. Bulgarian Split Squats

**Purpose**: Single-leg strength, balance, glute/quad development

**Setup**:
- Rear foot elevated on bench (laces down)
- Front foot 2-3 feet in front of bench
- Dumbbell in hand OPPOSITE to working leg (for balance)
- Torso upright

**Execution**:
- **3 seconds down**: Lower until front knee at 90°, back knee hovers above ground
- **0 second pause**: No pause at bottom
- **3 seconds up**: Drive through front heel to return to start
- Complete 8 reps, switch legs

**Current Load**: 25 lbs dumbbell

**Cues**:
- "Front shin stays vertical, knee tracks over toes"
- "Torso stays upright, don't lean forward"
- "Push through front heel"
- "Feel it in front leg glute and quad"

**Modifications**:
- If balance is difficult: Use TRX strap or hold wall for support
- If too easy: Increase dumbbell weight by 5 lbs
- Alternative: Reverse lunge (if rear-foot elevation bothers knee)

**Progression Plan**:
- Weeks 1-4: 25 lbs
- Weeks 5-8: 30 lbs
- Weeks 9-12: 35 lbs
- Weeks 13-16: Maintain 35 lbs

---

### 4. Single Leg Deadlift (Romanian Deadlift)

**Purpose**: Posterior chain (hamstrings, glutes), balance, hip stability

**Setup**:
- Stand on one leg
- Dumbbell in hand OPPOSITE to standing leg
- Slight bend in standing knee

**Execution**:
- **3 seconds down**: Hinge at hip, lower dumbbell toward ground while extending free leg behind
- **0 second pause**: Brief touch at bottom
- **2 seconds up**: Drive through standing heel, squeeze glute to return to start
- Complete 8 reps, switch legs

**Current Load**: 20 lbs dumbbell

**Cues**:
- "Hinge from hips, not rounding back"
- "Keep standing leg slightly bent"
- "Feel stretch in hamstring of standing leg"
- "Squeeze glute at top"

**Modifications**:
- If balance is difficult: Lightly touch wall or use nearby support
- If too easy: Increase weight by 5 lbs
- Alternative: Regular RDL (both feet on ground)

**Progression Plan**:
- Weeks 1-4: 20 lbs
- Weeks 5-8: 25 lbs
- Weeks 9-12: 30 lbs
- Weeks 13-16: Maintain 30 lbs

---

### 5. Banded Lateral Steps

**Purpose**: Hip abductor (glute medius) strengthening, knee stability

**Setup**:
- Resistance band around legs (just above or below knees)
- Athletic stance (slight squat, knees bent)
- Maintain tension in band throughout

**Execution**:
- Step laterally one direction (15 steps)
- Maintain squat position, don't stand up between steps
- Keep tension in band (knees stay apart)
- Return same direction or reverse direction for 15 steps

**Current Resistance**: Black band (medium-heavy)

**Cues**:
- "Stay low in athletic stance"
- "Push knees apart against band"
- "Don't let knees cave inward"
- "Feel it in side of hips (glute medius)"

**Modifications**:
- If too difficult: Lighter band or reduce steps to 10
- If too easy: Heavier band or increase steps to 20
- Alternative: Clamshells or side-lying leg raises

**Progression Plan**:
- Weeks 1-6: Black band, 15 steps x 2
- Weeks 7-12: Black band, 20 steps x 2 OR heavier band
- Weeks 13-16: Maintain week 7-12 protocol

---

### 6. Hamstring Curl Machine

**Purpose**: Hamstring strength, knee stability, balance quad dominance

**Setup**:
- Lie face down on hamstring curl machine
- Pad positioned just above ankles
- Align knee joint with machine pivot point

**Execution**:
- **2 seconds up**: Curl heels toward glutes
- **5 seconds hold**: Hold at top with maximum contraction
- **3 seconds down**: Lower slowly with control (eccentric emphasis)
- Complete 8 reps x 2 sets

**Current Load**: 100 lbs

**Cues**:
- "Pull heels to glutes, full range of motion"
- "Hold and squeeze at top - feel the burn"
- "Control the lowering, don't let it drop"
- "Keep hips pressed into pad"

**Modifications**:
- If too difficult: Reduce weight by 10-20 lbs
- If too easy: Add 10 lbs or increase hold time to 7 seconds
- Alternative: Nordic hamstring curls (bodyweight, advanced)

**Progression Plan**:
- Weeks 1-4: 100 lbs
- Weeks 5-8: 110 lbs
- Weeks 9-12: 120 lbs
- Weeks 13-16: Maintain 120 lbs

## Plyometric Progression (Integrated with HSR)

**Purpose**: Tendon loading, power development, running-specific strength

**IMPORTANT**: Only add plyometrics when ready (no acute knee pain, HSR established for 4+ weeks)

### Phase 1: Weeks 1-4 (Low-Level Plyometrics)

**NOT YET - Focus on HSR only**

### Phase 2: Weeks 5-8 (Introduce Low-Level Plyos)

**Add to Thursday HSR session** (after main HSR work):

**1. Pogo Hops** (3 x 20 reps)
- Minimal knee bend, bouncing on balls of feet
- Quick ground contact
- Focus on ankle stiffness

**2. Ankle Hops** (3 x 15 reps per leg)
- Single leg, minimal knee bend
- Quick, light contacts

**Total Volume**: ~100 ground contacts

**Add to Thursday HSR session**:

**1. Single Leg Hops** (3 x 10 per leg)
- Hop forward on one leg

- Step up onto box, drive knee up explosively
- Controlled step down

**3. Lateral Bounds** (3 x 8 per side)
- Bound laterally from one leg to other
- Stick landings

**Total Volume**: ~150 ground contacts

## Knee Pain Management Guidelines

### Pain Monitoring During HSR

**0-2/10 Pain**: Proceed as planned
**3-4/10 Pain**: Complete session but reduce weight by 10-15%
**5-6/10 Pain**: Reduce weight by 20-25%, modify range of motion
**7+/10 Pain**: Stop session, rest, consider medical consultation

### Exercise-Specific Modifications for Pain

**If Decline Squats cause pain >4/10**:
- Reduce weight by 20 lbs
- Reduce range of motion (don't go as deep)
- Switch to flat-ground squats (remove decline)
- Focus on other exercises, reduce decline squat volume

**If Bulgarian Splits cause pain**:
- Switch to reverse lunges (both feet on ground)
- Reduce weight
- Reduce range of motion

**General Rule**: HSR should cause muscle fatigue, not joint pain. Some tendon discomfort (2-3/10) is acceptable and part of the adaptation process.

## Session Timing & Recovery

### When to Schedule HSR

**Preferred Days**: Monday and Thursday

**Optimal Timing**:
1. After an easy run (30-60 min after completing run)
2. On a rest day from running (mid-morning or afternoon)
3. NEVER before a hard run or long run

**Recovery Between Sessions**:
- Minimum 48 hours between HSR sessions
- Minimum 24 hours before hard running workout
- Sauna session recommended after HSR (20 min)

### HSR + Running Integration

**Example Week**:
- **Monday**: Easy run (AM) + HSR (afternoon) + Sauna
- **Tuesday**: Easy run + Mobility
- **Wednesday**: Workout day (tempo/hills)
- **Thursday**: Easy run (AM) + HSR (afternoon) + Sauna
- **Friday**: Rest or easy run
- **Saturday**: Long run
- **Sunday**: Rest + mobility

## Common Questions

**"Can I do HSR on the same day as hard running workouts?"**
No. HSR after hard runs will compromise recovery. Schedule HSR after easy runs or on rest days.

**"What if I miss an HSR session?"**
No problem. Continue with next scheduled session. Don't try to "make up" missed sessions.

**"How do I know when to increase weight?"**
If you can complete all sets/reps with RPE 6-7/10 (moderate difficulty) for 2 consecutive sessions, increase weight by 5-10 lbs.

**"Should HSR cause muscle soreness?"**
Yes, especially initially. Muscle soreness (DOMS) for 24-48 hours is normal. Joint pain is not normal.

**"Can I add more exercises?"**
Focus on quality over quantity. These 6 exercises target all critical areas. Adding more may compromise recovery.

---

**Remember**: HSR training is a long-term investment in tendon health and injury prevention. Consistency over 16 weeks will build tendon resilience that supports your running goals.
