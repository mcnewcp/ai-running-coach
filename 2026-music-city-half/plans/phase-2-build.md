## Phase Overview

### Where You Are

Phase 1 built a strong foundation:
- Weekly mileage grew from 12 → 19 mpw (~88 total miles)
- Long run extended from 5.0 → 8.18 miles
- Zone 2 discipline is solid (avg HR 132-146 bpm on easy runs)

- Hill repeats handled confidently (7 x 60s)
- Knee pain well-managed (averaging 1-2/10)
- VO2 Max improved from 42.0 → 43.0+

### Primary Goals
1. **Volume Progression**: Build weekly mileage from 20 → 27 mpw safely
2. **Tempo Development**: Extend threshold work from 20 → 30 minutes
3. **Long Run Extension**: Progress from 9 → 11 miles, introduce race-effort finishes
4. **Strength-Endurance**: Longer hill repeats (90s vs 60s) for running economy
5. **Race Simulation**: First progressive long run with miles at half marathon effort
6. **Injury Prevention**: Continue HSR, maintain knee health through increased load

### Key Principles
- **80/20 Rule**: Maintained — 1 hard workout per week (Wednesday), long run on Saturday, everything else easy
- **No Consecutive Hard Days**: Wednesday workout → Thursday easy. Saturday long run → Sunday rest.
- **Cutback Week**: Week 10 reduces volume ~20% for adaptation before the biggest week
- **Knee Monitoring**: Same protocol — <3/10 proceed, 4-6/10 modify, 7+/10 rest
- **Zone 2 Discipline**: Easy runs stay easy (<150 bpm). Tempo and hill days are the only hard days.

### What's New in Phase 2
- **Longer tempo runs**: 25 → 30 min (up from 20 min in Phase 1)
- **Longer hill repeats**: 90-second efforts (up from 60-second in Phase 1)
- **Progressive long run**: Last 3 miles at half marathon effort (Week 11)
- **Higher weekly volume**: 5-6 runs per week, longer easy runs (35-45 min)
- **More total easy miles**: Easy runs grow from ~2.5-3.0 mi to ~3.0-4.0 mi

## Common Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge: "My easy pace hasn't gotten faster"
**Solution**: Aerobic development takes 8-12 weeks to show pace improvements at the same HR. You're still in that window. Trust the process — your 8-mile run already felt easier than your 7-miler at similar effort. That IS improvement.

### Challenge: "30 minutes of tempo feels long"
**Solution**: Break it into mental chunks (3 x 10 min). The first 10 min is finding rhythm, middle 10 is holding, last 10 is where you get stronger. It's OK if pace slows slightly in the final minutes — sustaining the effort is what matters.

### Challenge: "I'm nervous about 10+ mile long runs"
**Solution**: You ran 8.18 miles and said you could have gone further. The jump from 8 to 10 is manageable because your aerobic base is now solid. Walk breaks are fine. Fuel properly. You'll surprise yourself.

### Challenge: "Knee pain is creeping up with the higher volume"
**Solution**: This is the most important signal to monitor. If pain is trending above 3/10 consistently:
1. First: Ensure HSR is consistent and progressive
2. Second: Reduce easy run volume by 15-20% (keep the quality sessions)
3. Third: Add an extra rest day (drop to 4 runs/week)
4. Fourth: If still trending up, extend Week 10 cutback to 2 weeks

### Challenge: "I feel tired all the time"
**Solution**: Check these in order:
1. Sleep: Are you getting 7.5+ hours consistently?
2. Nutrition: Are you eating enough to fuel the increased mileage?
3. Life stress: Has something changed outside of training?
4. Overtraining: Is your resting HR elevated 5+ bpm above baseline?
If multiple factors are off, take an extra easy day. One missed run is better than a burned-out week.

### Challenge: "I want to run the long run faster"
**Solution**: The progressive long run in Week 11 is your chance to run faster — at the end, on tired legs, which is exactly what the half marathon demands. Until then, bank those Zone 2 miles. Fast long runs are the #1 cause of injury in half marathon training.

## Nutrition & Fueling Notes for Phase 2

As mileage increases and long runs extend past 90 minutes, fueling becomes a training skill:

### During Long Runs (9+ miles)
- **Water**: Every 2-3 miles (carry a handheld or plan a route with water access)
- **Fuel**: 30-60g carbs per hour after the first 45-60 minutes
  - Options: Energy gels, dates, banana pieces, sports drink
  - **Test everything in training** — nothing new on race day
- **Pre-run meal**: 2-3 hours before, moderate carbs + light protein (e.g., oatmeal + banana, toast + peanut butter)

### Daily Nutrition
- Ensure adequate protein for recovery (0.7-1g per lb body weight = ~120-170g/day)
- Carbs are fuel — don't restrict them during build phase
- Continue low sodium approach for BP management
- Hydrate consistently throughout the day, not just around runs
