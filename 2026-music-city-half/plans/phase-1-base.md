# Phase 1: Base Building (Weeks 1-6)

**Duration**: January 6 - February 16, 2026
**Objective**: Build aerobic foundation, establish Zone 2 discipline, strengthen tendons
**Mileage Range**: 12-19 miles per week
**Long Run Progression**: 5 → 8 miles

---

## Phase Overview

### Primary Goals
1. **Aerobic Development**: Build mitochondrial density and aerobic capacity through Zone 2 training
2. **Zone 2 Mastery**: Develop discipline to keep HR <150 bpm on all easy runs
3. **Tendon Strengthening**: Consistent HSR training to manage patellar tendinopathy
4. **Movement Patterns**: Establish pre-run, post-run, and recovery routines
5. **Progressive Loading**: Safely increase weekly mileage from 12 → 19 mpw

### Key Principles
- **80/20 Rule**: All runs are easy (Zone 2) until Week 3, then 80% easy
- **Knee Protection**: Monitor pain daily, <3/10 to proceed
- **No Consecutive Hard Days**: Weeks 3-6 hard workout followed by easy or rest
- **Cutback Week**: Week 4 reduces volume 20% for adaptation

## Common Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge: "I feel too slow in Zone 2"
**Solution**: This is expected and correct. Zone 2 builds your aerobic engine. Speed comes later from improved efficiency. Trust the process - your pace will improve at the same HR over time.

### Challenge: "My HR spikes at the start of runs"
**Solution**: Very normal. Start even slower for the first 10-15 minutes. Your HR will settle as your body warms up. Consider longer warm-up periods.

### Challenge: "Knee pain increased after long run"
**Solution**: This is expected (mild pain 3-4/10 day after long run is OK). Monitor trend - if pain stays elevated >3/10 for 48+ hours, reduce next week's mileage by 20%. Always prioritize knee health.

### Challenge: "I missed a workout due to life/weather"
**Solution**: No problem. Skip it and move on - do NOT try to "make it up." Better to miss one session than risk injury or fatigue from cramming.

### Challenge: "Strides feel awkward"
**Solution**: They'll feel more natural with practice. Focus on form, not speed. Think "smooth and controlled," not "fast sprinting."

### Challenge: "I want to run more miles"
**Solution**: Resist this urge. Progressive overload is about patience. More mileage now = injury risk. Build gradually and you'll get there safely.
