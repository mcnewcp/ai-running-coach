# Patellar Tendinopathy Rehab Block — 12 Weeks, May 25 to Aug 16, 2026

Strength-led rehab block focused on remodeling the left patellar tendon via HSR (Heavy Slow Resistance), with conservative symptom-gated running. Replaces the second half of the originally planned off-season base phase ([2026-05-11-off-season-base.md](2026-05-11-off-season-base.md) ended early at May 24).

Built on a 12-week plan developed with deep-research input on patellar loading, hip control, and progressive rehab.

## Why this block exists

Two weeks into the off-season base, the left knee was sitting at **3/10 post-Saturday-long-run** with a rising trend across the week — a yellow flag, not yet red, but enough to act on before it became a forced layoff. Continued volume progression on a still-irritated tendon is the path to a chronic problem. Pivoting to **load-it-don't-rest-it** with HSR is the evidence-based path to durable tendon health.

Goal: arrive at Aug 17 with a symptom-free patellar tendon, a verified-improving decline squat score, a meaningful strength baseline across the 5-slot template, and ~24 mpw of clean Z2 volume under the belt.

## Guiding principles

1. **Strength first, running second.** Three lifting sessions a week is the treatment. Running rebuilds in parallel but at a conservative cadence.
2. **No high-strain-rate loading for 12 weeks.** No hills, no intervals, no sprints, no downhill running, no plyometrics. Zone 2 only.
3. **Symptoms steer the bus, the calendar is a default.** The single-leg decline squat test is the weekly objective progress marker.
4. **Continue everything that's working.** Sauna 4x/week, mobility, sleep priority.
5. **Glute medius is part of the prescription, not optional.** Both a Slot 4 (main) and Slot 5 (finisher) every session.

## The Progression Rule (most important section)

**Advance to the next week's running volume only if both are true:**

1. The previous week was **symptom-free during runs** (pain ≤ 1/10 during running)
2. The **morning-after pain on the single-leg decline squat test was ≤ 2/10**

If either condition fails, **repeat the previous week.** If pain is *rising* despite repeating, **drop back one week.**

Tendinopathy pain often shows up 24 hours after the load that caused it. A run that feels fine can produce next-morning soreness. The morning check matters more than the in-run check. **The calendar is a default; symptoms are the steering wheel.**

This rule applies to **running volume only**. The strength progression follows its own pain rule (≤3/10 during lifting, settles by next morning) and is generally robust — strength sessions are the treatment, not the problem.

## The Single-Leg Decline Squat Test

Full protocol in [resources/hsr-protocols.md](../../../resources/hsr-protocols.md). Quick reference:

- **Once per week, same day** (recommend Saturday morning).
- **Done fresh** — not after a workout or run. Mornings ideal.
- 5 reps per leg, 25° decline, 5-second eccentric, target ~60° knee flexion.
- Rate worst pain at lower edge of kneecap, 0–10.
- **0–2/10** = green light to progress. **3–4/10** = yellow, repeat current week. **5+/10** or rising = red, drop back; 2 weeks persistent = clinical evaluation.

A reduction in this score over time is the single best objective measure that the program is working.

## What to Track

Tracking stays light-touch (off-season cadence) — **no daily morning metrics intake**. The goal is sustainability; metric fatigue is itself a risk to plan adherence.

Track when it's load-relevant:

- **Runs** → `data/runs.csv` as always; knee rating reported with the run if notable
- **Lift sessions** → log in the weekly log file (date, slot picks, working load, sets × reps, any in-lift pain >2/10)
- **Morning-after pain** → log it only if it was meaningful (>2/10) the day after a lift or long run. No baseline-required entry on benign days.
- **Weekly single-leg decline squat score** (both legs, Saturday morning, fresh) — **this is the one structured weekly metric**. It's the objective progress marker for the whole block.
- **Sauna / mobility** → logged as before

If something feels off (energy, sleep, knee trending up), report it. Otherwise no structured daily intake.

## Pain Rules (summary)

### Acceptable
- Up to **3/10 during lifting** that settles by next morning
- Up to **1/10 during running**
- Mild morning stiffness that resolves within a few minutes

### Adjust the plan
- **3–4/10** on the weekly decline squat test → repeat current running volume
- Pain **worsening across consecutive sessions of the same lift** → drop one RM step
- Asymmetry persisting past week 6 → emphasize weaker side, train it first

### Stop and seek clinical evaluation
- Pain **>5/10 during running**
- **Swelling** at or around the patellar tendon
- Pain **at rest** or pain that wakes you at night
- **No improvement** in decline squat score after 6 consecutive weeks of compliant training
- A sudden **pop, snap, or sharp tearing sensation** during any activity

## After Week 12 — Reassessment Criteria

If symptom-free at week 12 with a clean decline squat score (≤ 2/10 on affected side, ideally matching unaffected), the next block can reintroduce:

- **Strides** (short, controlled, on flat ground)
- **Gentle rolling terrain**
- **Light tempo work** (one session per week)

Hold off on **hill repeats, intervals, dedicated downhill running** until **4+ consecutive weeks of full training volume with no symptom flare**.

Glute medius work continues indefinitely (Slot 4 + Slot 5 each session) — 10-minute insurance policy with good evidence.
